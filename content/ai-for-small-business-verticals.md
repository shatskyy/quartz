To justify the implementation of a connective [[AI]] layer, a [[small business vertical]] must meet several criteria:

1.  **Digital Exhaust & Fragmentation**: The work should already pass through multiple specialized systems, generating significant "legible artifacts" (e.g., reservations, invoices, schedules, reviews). This creates cross-tool fragmentation, not just a single platform. These artifacts make a business "queryable."
2.  **Value at Stake**: There must be substantial value to unlock through [[automation]] or intelligence. This translates into clear monetization opportunities like lower labor costs, fewer missed leads, faster approvals, fewer no-shows, better utilization, or improved customer retention.
3.  **Existing System-of-Record**: A real system-of-record stack must already be in place, even if the specific tools vary by sector. Vertical SaaS solutions are successful here because work happens within industry-specific platforms.
4.  **Buyer Proximity to Operations**: The decision-maker for tooling (e.g., owner-operators, GMs, department heads) should be close to daily operations rather than buried in enterprise procurement processes.

Common verticals exhibiting these traits include restaurants, hospitality, law, home services, e-commerce, and creative production. [[AI in Home Services]] is an example of a sector particularly well-suited for such an application.
