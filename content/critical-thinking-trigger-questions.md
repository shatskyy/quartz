# Critical Thinking and Trigger Questions

Developing robust critical thinking skills, especially when transitioning into fields like [[tech management]] or [[product management]], hinges on **metacognition**—the ability to think about one's own thinking process. This involves consciously challenging [[orthodox patterns of thinking]] and actively seeking new perspectives.

A powerful technique for achieving this is the use of **trigger questions**. These questions are designed to disrupt assumptions and encourage deeper inquiry. Instead of accepting information at face value, one might ask questions that others typically overlook, fostering a mindset akin to that of [[geniuses]] who master this art.

Fundamental trigger questions include:
*   How do A and B connect?
*   What evidence supports this claim?
*   Is this unique, or is it a development of something else?

These questions can be applied across various perspectives:
*   **Material/Environmental**: Considering practical needs and environmental impacts.
*   **Individual**: Exploring psychological and intellectual factors.
*   **Social**: Analyzing economic, political, and cultural influences.

The "What if" question is particularly potent, serving as a foundation for [[original thinking]]. By exploring hypotheticals, one opens doors to unique answers and innovative solutions, moving beyond conventional boundaries.
