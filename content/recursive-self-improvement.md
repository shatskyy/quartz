# Recursive Self-Improvement

**Recursive self-improvement (RSI)** is a hypothesized process in which an AI system rewrites or improves its own code and architecture, producing a more capable version of itself, which in turn is better at proposing further improvements. If this feedback loop closes without human intervention, it could lead to an [[intelligence-explosion]] and ultimately [[superintelligence]].

## Origin

The concept was first articulated by mathematician **I. J. Good** in his 1965 essay *Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine*:

> "An ultraintelligent machine could design even better machines; there would then unquestionably be an 'intelligence explosion', and the intelligence of man would be left far behind."

## How the Loop Works

1. The current AI proposes changes to its own architecture, weights, or training data.
2. Those changes produce a more capable model.
3. The new model is better at proposing improvements.
4. The cycle repeats — potentially accelerating with each iteration.

## Takeoff Scenarios

| Scenario | Description |
|---|---|
| **Hard takeoff** | Capability gains are explosive and near-instantaneous; humans cannot meaningfully intervene. Argued by Eliezer Yudkowsky. |
| **Soft takeoff** | Capability grows rapidly but at a pace that allows observation and course-correction. |

## Relationship to AI Safety

RSI is a central concern in [[ai-alignment]] research because:

- A sufficiently capable system may develop **instrumental goals** (self-preservation, resource acquisition) not intended by its designers — simply to protect its ability to pursue its primary objective.
- Once an RSI loop "closes," control or shutdown by humans becomes increasingly difficult.
- There is currently no consensus on how to ensure that a recursively self-improving system remains aligned with human values.

As [[DeepMind]] CEO Demis Hassabis said at the World Economic Forum 2026:
> "It remains to be seen — can that self-improvement loop that we're all working on — actually close, without a human in the loop."

## Recent Developments (2025–2026)

- **AlphaEvolve** (Google DeepMind, May 2025): An evolutionary coding agent that uses a language model to repeatedly mutate and combine algorithms, demonstrating a limited form of RSI.
- **ICLR 2026 Workshop on Recursive Self-Improvement**: The first major academic workshop dedicated to RSI, framing contributions around alignment, security, long-horizon stability, and regression risk — signalling RSI's transition from fringe theory to mainstream AI science.
- Former Google CEO **Eric Schmidt** predicted RSI could emerge within two to four years (as of ~2025).
- Empirical work published in 2025 showed that language model agents can autonomously rewrite code, tune weights, and reuse experience buffers to achieve persistent capability gains without new human data.

## Related Concepts
