# Cognitive Filters

Cognitive filters represent the inherent biases and preconceived notions through which individuals process and interpret incoming information. These filters are shaped by one's unique [[experience]]s and can significantly distort understanding, especially in nuanced [[Communication]].

Instead of making predictions or assumptions about what another person will say, it is more effective to allow them to speak freely. This approach minimizes the 'pollution' of incoming information with one's own internal filters, thereby preventing gaps in understanding.

While these filters are natural, their potentially harmful effects can be mitigated through practices such as [[Active Listening]], paraphrasing, and seeking clarification from the counterparty. These techniques create opportunities to identify and correct misinterpretations, leading to a more accurate and shared understanding.
