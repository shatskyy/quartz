Transitioning into a tech career requires a structured approach to learning and skill development. These strategies can help in navigating the complexities of acquiring new expertise and adapting to industry demands.

## 1. Define the Problem and Identify Gaps

Before diving into learning, clearly articulate the demands of target tech roles (e.g., Product Manager, Growth Engineer, Product Analyst). [[Problem Solving]] is key here.
*   **Trigger Questions**: Use specific questions to narrow down and understand role requirements.
*   **Skill Audit**: Compare current skills and workflows (e.g., from capital markets) against those required by tech job descriptions. Identify specific [[Skill Gaps]].

## 2. Utilize the T-Model for Strategic Research

Adopt a T-model approach for effective research:
*   **Broad Skimming**: Skim industry reports and introductory texts to gain a wide understanding.
*   **Deep Reading**: Focus on core competencies through word-to-word analysis and deep reading.
*   **Primary Sources**: Prioritize technical documentation and direct case studies over blogs for reliable information.

## 3. Metacognition and Planning

Engage in [[Metacognition]] by working backward from your career goals.
*   **Time Blocking**: Allocate specific daily sessions for focused study.
*   **Active Recall**: Employ [[Active Recall]] techniques by testing yourself on concepts and sketching solutions to product problems, rather than passive review.

## 4. Construct New Knowledge and Be a Creator

Move beyond consumption to creation.
*   **Research Projects**: Undertake small-scale research projects and build technical products to apply learning.
*   **Showcase Capabilities**: Document and showcase research involvement and [[Problem Solving]] skills.
*   **Critical Evaluation**: Evaluate existing tech successes using proven frameworks and justify why growth strategies might or might not apply in different contexts.

## 5. Networking as Research

Approach [[Networking]] as a research activity to gather insights.
*   **Terms of Reference**: Note underlying industry assumptions and terms of reference when speaking with professionals.
*   **Structured Discussions**: Prepare a written list of discussion points and questions, and take detailed notes during conversations.

## Cognitive Flexibility

A high level of [[Cognitive Flexibility]] is crucial for adapting thinking as situations and industry landscapes evolve.
